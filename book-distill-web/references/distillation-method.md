# Distillation method

## Evidence ledger

Maintain an evidence ledger while reading. Each row records: source anchor, chapter or section, compact claim, evidence or example, relationship to the main argument, confidence, and notes.

Maintain a separate coverage ledger with one row per substantive page range or reflowable section: `range`, `chapter_id`, `status` (`read`, `unreadable`, `excluded`, or `pending`), extraction quality, and notes. Only a ledger with no unexplained `pending` ranges can support `coverage: complete`.

Use confidence labels: `high` for explicit anchored claims; `medium` for synthesis from multiple anchored passages; `low` for uncertain extraction or indirect inference. Confidence never replaces an anchor.

## Map pass

Recover the book's internal logic before evaluating it. Identify its motivating problem, intended reader, central claim, argumentative sequence, special terms, and the examples or studies doing the argumentative work. Write a one-sentence role for every chapter, then reconstruct each chapter's question, main line, subarguments, mechanisms, evidence/examples, qualifications, conclusion, and transitions in source order.

## Compare pass

Compare claims across chapters. Record repeated spine claims, concepts whose meanings shift, causal links and feedback loops, internal tensions, evidence gaps, and boundary conditions such as industry, culture, company stage, role, or time period. Critique the argument actually made.

## Distill pass

A faithful distillation lets the reader answer:

- What does the author want me to see differently?
- How do the major ideas connect?
- What evidence supports them?
- Where might they fail?
- What can I observe or do next?

For each core concept, assign a stable ID and include a compact definition, deeper explanation, factors, consequences, observables or application, boundary, role in the argument, anchors, and relationships to other concept IDs. For each action, state trigger, action, expected signal, and review question.

## Excerpt policy

- Use short verbatim excerpts when the exact phrase defines a term, states a central claim, or preserves a memorable formulation.
- Copy the source exactly, verify it visually when extraction is doubtful, and attach a precise anchor.
- Surround each excerpt with explanation: what it means, why it matters here, and how it connects to the argument.
- Do not stitch separated fragments into a quotation or silently repair uncertain text.
- Do not reproduce long runs of prose. A distillation should remain an original analytical work grounded in the source.

## Integrity rules

- Do not call a source fully read unless all substantive pages or chapters were processed.
- A table of contents or synopsis is orientation material, not evidence for full coverage.
- Separate description from evaluation and evaluation from recommendation.
- Preserve contradictions and uncertainty when the source does not resolve them.
- Use external sources only when requested; cite them separately.
- Do not fill source gaps with general domain knowledge. Mark pending concepts and return to them after their source sections are read.
